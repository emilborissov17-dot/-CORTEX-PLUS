# T1 result — does retrieving the system's own verified past work help on a new task?

**Verdict: FAIL.**

Pre-registration: `claude/reports/PREREG_TRANSFER_2026-09-17.md`, committed `0061c68`
before any arm ran; embedder addendum `b8b3dd5`. Harness `tools/transfer_test.py`
(`d39a276`), tasks `tools/transfer_tasks.py` (`f6c8195`).

**A pass on synthetic tasks demonstrates the mechanism only.**

Run 17 September 2026, `qwen3:8b`, `think=False`, temperature 0.0, top-k 3, 64 tasks ×
3 arms = 192 calls. Task ledger sha256
`cd574b575f855c055e1ea473a46aae654bbfab58f31d5417d365cc8a2bd660fa`.

---

## 1. The run is valid

| | on | off | shuffled |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| parse failures | **0** | **0** | **0** |
| transport errors | **0** | **0** | **0** |

192 of 192 trials produced a usable direction and probability. No trial was dropped,
none was retried, no arm was imputed. The Brier comparison is **not** void — parse-failure
rates are identical across arms.

This is a null result from a working experiment, not a broken one.

## 2. Success rate

| arm | success | Brier |
|---|---:|---:|
| memory-on | 0.7812 | 0.3150 |
| memory-off | **0.7969** | **0.3042** |
| shuffled | 0.7188 | 0.3328 |

**Memory-on did not beat memory-off. It was 1.6 points worse.**

Constant-guess baselines on this task set: always-UP 0.5781, always-DOWN 0.4219. All
three arms are well above both, so the model is doing real work on the series — it is
retrieval that adds nothing.

## 3. Decomposition, as pre-registered

```
total               on − off        = −0.0156   95% CI [−0.1250, +0.0938]
retrieval-specific  on − shuffled   = +0.0625   95% CI [−0.0469, +0.1719]
format              shuffled − off  = −0.0781   95% CI [−0.1719, +0.0156]
```

Paired bootstrap, 10,000 resamples over tasks, n = 64. **Every interval contains zero.**

The only positive point estimate is on − shuffled (+6.25 pp), and its CI crosses zero.
Read carefully, it does not say retrieval helped: shuffled is the *worst* arm, and
memory-off — which contains no episodes at all — beat both. The most that can be said is
that irrelevant episodes may hurt slightly more than relevant ones do.

## 4. Acceptance conditions

| # | condition | required | measured | met? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | on − off ≥ 15 pp, CI excludes 0 | ≥ +0.15 | −0.0156, CI [−0.125, +0.094] | **NO** |
| 2 | on − shuffled ≥ 7.5 pp, CI excludes 0 | ≥ +0.075 | +0.0625, CI [−0.047, +0.172] | **NO** |
| 3 | benefit tracks latent > surface | ρ_latent > 0 and > ρ_surface | ρ_latent = **−0.0592**, ρ_surface = **+0.2137** | **NO** |

**Zero of three.** Condition 3 fails in the specific direction the prereg named as
"lookup by resemblance": per-task benefit correlates with *surface* similarity (+0.21)
and slightly *negatively* with latent similarity (−0.06). With no overall benefit to
explain, that correlation is weak evidence about a small effect — but it points away
from transfer, not toward it.

## 5. Hold-outs, reported separately

The stratum the design turns on: compositions absent from memory, built from primitives
memory has.

| stratum | n | on | off | shuffled |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| single | 24 | 0.8750 | 0.8750 | 0.7500 |
| seen_composition | 24 | 0.7500 | 0.7500 | 0.6250 |
| **holdout_composition** | **16** | **0.6875** | **0.7500** | **0.8125** |

```
hold-outs:  on − off       = −0.0625   95% CI [−0.2500, +0.1250]
            on − shuffled  = −0.1250   95% CI [−0.3750, +0.1250]
```

**On hold-outs, memory-on is the worst arm and shuffled is the best.** Both intervals
contain zero and n = 16 is small, so this is not evidence that retrieval *hurts*. It is
the clearest possible absence of the effect the experiment was built to detect: in the
one stratum where transfer would have to show up, the ordering is reversed.

Note also that on and off are *identical* on the two non-hold-out strata (0.8750 and
0.7500). Retrieval changed the answer on those exactly zero times in aggregate.

## 6. What actually happened, mechanically

- `on` and `off` gave the **same direction on 51 of 64 tasks**. The retrieved episodes
  changed the model's answer on 13 tasks, and those changes were net slightly negative.
- The three arms disagreed on 17 of 64 tasks.
- Mean stated probability: on 0.8688, off 0.7945, shuffled 0.8516. **Retrieval made the
  model more confident without making it more accurate** — which is why its Brier is
  worse than the arm with no memory at all.
- Retrieval itself worked as measured: mean latent similarity of the top hit 0.5495
  against mean surface similarity 0.0807, matching the standalone measurement exactly.
  The episodes reaching the prompt were structurally relevant. They just did not help.

## 7. Verdict against the prereg

**FAIL.** Zero of three acceptance conditions met.

The prereg's own reading for this outcome, written before the run:

> *no arm separates* — On this grammar, retrieved past work does not help. Reported as
> a null. It does not prove the real memory layer is useless — it does remove the main
> reason to assume it is useful.

That is the finding, and it stands unsoftened. No re-cut, no subgroup rescue, no
"promising trend". The one positive point estimate in the whole table has a CI crossing
zero and sits beside a negative total.

## 8. Where I think the design was weak — stated, not acted on

The prereg says that if I think the design was wrong I say so and stop, and that a second
run needs a new pre-registration. So this is a statement, not a change.

**There was very little headroom.** memory-off scored 0.7969. The ceiling is 1.0, so the
largest possible on − off was +20.3 pp, against a required +15 pp. The experiment
demanded that retrieval capture three-quarters of the remaining error. A task set the
model can already do at 80% cannot show much benefit from anything, and the 15-point
threshold was set without knowing the no-memory baseline.

That is a real limitation and it cuts one way only: **it could hide a true effect, it
could not manufacture one.** The FAIL is not explained away by it. But a future design
should fix the baseline nearer 0.5 — longer series, noisier draws, or a question the
model cannot answer by inspecting eight numbers — and declare the threshold relative to
the measured headroom rather than in absolute points.

Two further limits, both recorded before the run:

- **The embedder is `qwen2.5:3b`, a generative model, not an embedding model.** The
  approved `nomic-embed-text` pull failed: the ollama daemon is denied outbound by a
  host firewall rule (registry reachable from Python at the same moment; general
  outbound fine). Selection fell to the addendum's second clause — no change without
  evidence. Hold-out recall@1 was 0.625, so relevant episodes *were* being retrieved;
  a better embedder would raise that, not change the fact that retrieved-and-relevant
  episodes did not help. **Outstanding, not declined.**
- **Synthetic tasks from a grammar this repo wrote.** Whatever this shows, it shows
  about the mechanism.

## 10. What this changes

The knowledge layer has no demonstrated consumer. That was the question, and the answer
today is no.

It does not follow that the layer is worthless — this tested one retrieval mechanism, on
one synthetic grammar, with a model that was already good at the task. It does follow
that **building more of it on the assumption that retrieval will pay is now unsupported**,
and that the next thing built should be the experiment that could show a benefit, not
more storage.

Statements work remains paused, as instructed. `canon_invariants` remains 0 and the
`lesson_key()` exact-string finding remains untouched, for after T1.

**A pass on synthetic tasks demonstrates the mechanism only** — and this was not a pass.
