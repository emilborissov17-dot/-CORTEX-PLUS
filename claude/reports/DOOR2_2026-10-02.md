## What does not work

1. **Each `openclaw browser …` call costs about 5–6 s of CLI overhead** while the gateway handles the request in
   70–483 ms (OpenClaw's own log). A fetch through the CLI cannot get below ~15 s; through one long-lived
   loopback connection to the same Chrome it took 0.13–0.28 s (Step 3c). Using that path needs a new module in
   `config/network_allowlist.json` — **Emil's decision; nothing is wired.**
2. **The false "exited before adoption" and the stop that leaves Chrome are still there — survived, not
   removed.** Their cause is named (below) and lies inside OpenClaw/Chrome, not in a timeout; OpenClaw's
   configuration was not changed (Step 3b's condition did not hold). Why the Chrome that OpenClaw spawns
   hands over to another process on this machine: **UNKNOWN** (looked at: OpenClaw's log, its launch and
   stop code; not looked at: Chrome's own logs).
3. **The leftover guard has still not been met live** (Step 2): the situation "Chrome alive while status says
   not running" could not be produced — after a gateway restart the gateway re-detects the Chrome through its
   port (`running: true, cdpReady: true`). The guard is proven by tests only.
4. **A session holds one Chrome for the whole process** (§20 (1)): the readers' `http` face and
   `fetch_standard.get` keep it until the process exits (stopped by an exit handler). A process killed hard
   leaves its Chrome for the next start's leftover guard.
5. **A one-shot fetch still takes ~62 s** — start (with the false error and the 5 s wait) and stop (with the
   PID end) are paid every time; only a session or a long-lived connection removes that.
