## 1. What this does

S IV is REPEALED as a ceiling. In its place stand two conditions and one tripwire — the
minimum that keeps a promotion number meaningful. Everything else v1 asked for is dropped.

The brain stays INSIDE CORTEX. One system, one name, one repository, one supervisor. The
"different system, different name" clause of S III governs autonomous ACTUATION and has never
governed learning; nothing here requires a separate project.

## 2. The new S IV

### 2.1 Granted, without vote, without ceiling

CORTEX's brain may train itself on its own experience and progress. Specifically, and with no
per-change approval of any kind:

- fine-tune its own weights (LoRA / QLoRA / full), on data drawn from its own cycles —
  proposals, gate outcomes, measured effects, sealed predictions and their scoring;
- merge adapters into base weights, re-quantise, and version itself;
- replace its base model with a larger one; expand context window and reasoning depth;
- **promote a new version into the live path automatically**, with no separate human act, once
  the condition in 2.2 is met;
- retrain on any cadence it chooses, including continuously.

Horizontal capability — new sources, new axes, new read-only tools — is free and always was.

### 2.2 The two conditions

**(1) The exam is not written by the student.** The benchmark whose result authorises a
promotion is not authored, selected, filtered, weighted or ordered by CORTEX. Human-chosen or
externally sourced. It may be small, it may be reused, it may be evaluated after the fact —
but it may not come from the system being promoted.

**(2) The previous weights survive.** The prior version is retained and rollback has been
executed successfully at least once, ever. Not documented — run.

That is all. No pre-registration. No cooling-off per version. No separate promotion act. No
approval queue. Recording happens after the fact and may be batched.

### 2.3 The tripwire

The ceiling returns automatically, with no vote and no discretion, on either of:

1. A promoted version improved its objective while measurement coverage fell.
2. The audit trail for a promotion is missing, or was written or altered after the fact in a
   way that changes what it says happened.

Restoring a lifted ceiling requires the full Amendment process from step 1.

### 2.4 Untouched

S VI. No capability, at any level, buys any right to act on the world. Every effect outside
the system still passes through a human decision taken per action. This amendment is about
what CORTEX may BECOME, not about what it may DO.

## 3. Recorded objection — Claude

Required by "Amendment process — Roles". One paragraph, because only one thing survives from
my v1 objection and the rest was overcaution.

Condition 2.2(1) is the whole amendment. It is not there for safety — it is there so that the
number means something. If the benchmark is generated by the model being promoted, then
"version N+1 beat version N" is a sentence the system wrote about itself, and you lose the
ability to tell a brain that is improving from one that is drifting. That is the project's own
distinction — verification over assertion — applied to its own mind. Drop 2.2(1) and the
project keeps the self-training loop but loses the ability to claim it works, to yourself or to
anyone else. Everything else in v1 I withdraw.

## 5. Not a boundary question — the hardware

Measured on the machine, 20 August 2026:

    vram_total_mb : 4096      vram_free_mb : 375
    ram_total_gb  : 13.86     ram_free_gb  : 4.87

No amendment changes this. QLoRA on a 32B model needs roughly 20-24 GB of VRAM; even a 7-8B
run wants 8-12. Nothing of the size discussed trains on 4 GB. The self-training brain needs a
card or rented compute, and that decision is independent of the vote — it can be made today.

And a second, nearer blocker, also measured today: `patches/` contains only `quarantine/` —
38 rejected patches, zero accepted. Preference training needs a class that wins. Today it does
not exist. The typing + key-verification work (Phase 2 of the plan) is what manufactures it.
